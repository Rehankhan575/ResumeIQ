## 1. Resume Split (Classifier / Matcher / NER Gold)

### Source
- **Dataset**: LiveCareer (~2,484 rows, CC BY-SA 4.0)
- **After MIN_CHARS=200 filter**: 2,483 resumes
- **Categories**: 24 (`ACCOUNTANT`, `ADVOCATE`, … `TEACHER`)

### Grouping Strategy
Groups are formed by union-find over two criteria:

1. **Exact duplicate** `Resume_str` — identical byte sequences are merged.
2. **TF-IDF cosine ≥ 0.90** — `TfidfVectorizer(stop_words='english', max_features=10000, sublinear_tf=True)`, computed block-wise.

**Result**: 2,468 groups from 2,483 resumes (16 TF-IDF pairs merged). No mixed-category groups were found.

### Split
| Split | Resumes | Approx. % |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| train | 1,739   | 70.0%     |
| val   | 370     | 14.9%     |
| test  | 374     | 15.1%     |

Split performed at **group level**, stratified by majority category label per group. Implemented via two-step `train_test_split(random_state=42)`.

### Header Masking & Label Leak

LiveCareer resumes begin with a job-title header stored in
`<div class="paragraph PARAGRAPH_NAME firstparagraph">` (primary) or
`<div class="name">` (fallback). This header is extracted from HTML and
stripped from the leading text to produce `text_masked`.

Leak is measured as: fraction of resumes where the category label
(word-boundary regex) appears in the **first 10 words** of the text.

| Metric                        | Value  |
|-------------------------------|--------|
| Leak before masking (overall) | 65.1%  |
| Leak after masking (overall)  | 19.7%  |
| Median chars removed          | 359    |

**Target <10% not reached.** Residual leak is primarily due to job-function
words recurring throughout the resume body (e.g., "HR" in HR resumes, "Sales"
in Sales resumes). These are genuine semantic signals, not data-pipeline
artefacts. No further masking is applied to avoid destroying signal.

5 worst categories (before → after masking):

| Category             | Before | After  |
|----------------------|--------|--------|
| BUSINESS-DEVELOPMENT | 100.0% | 28.6%  |
| ENGINEERING          | 100.0% | 31.4%  |
| FINANCE              | 100.0% | 28.0%  |
| HR                   | 100.0% | 23.6%  |
| SALES                | 100.0% | 31.9%  |

> **Implication**: The classifier should be evaluated and reported with
> **both** `text` and `text_masked` to make the effect of label leak visible.

## 2. JD Split (Matcher Training Pool)

### Source
- **Dataset**: `linkedin_sample` (5,000 postings, `postings_5k.parquet`)
- **After dedup on (title, description[:200])**: 4,897 unique JDs
- **After title→category mapping**: 1,729 mapped JDs
- **Excluded**: 1,729 ambiguous + unmapped JDs

### Title→Category Mapping
Rules live in `data/taxonomies/title_category_map.csv` (244 keyword rules,
24 categories). Each title is matched case-insensitively on word boundaries
(`\b...\b`). Titles matching >1 category are **AMBIGUOUS** and excluded;
titles matching 0 categories are **UNMAPPED** and excluded.

### Split
| Split | JDs   | Approx. % |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| train | 1,210 | 70.0%     |
| val   | 259   | 15.0%     |
| test  | 260   | 15.0%     |

Split at (title, description[:200]) group level. Stratification attempted
but fell back to unstratified due to sparse categories (see below).

### Categories Too Small for Matcher (<20 mapped JDs)

The following categories have fewer than 20 mapped JDs total and are
**flagged `too_small_for_matcher`**:

| Category         | Total JDs |
|------------------|----------:|

| APPAREL          |        10 |
| ARTS             |         5 |

Matcher training and evaluation metrics for these categories should be

## 3. JRF Eval Set

**Source**: `job_resume_fit/train.parquet` (2,385 rows, 23 unique JDs).

Rows whose `resume_id` is in the classifier **test split** are extracted
as `data/processed/jrf_eval.parquet` for external validation only.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total rows in jrf_eval | 359 |
| Classifier test IDs covered | 359 / 374 |
| APPAREL resumes in test | 0 (none in JRF) |

**Usage**: Compute Spearman rank correlation between matcher semantic score
and `ai_match_score` on these rows. Do **not** train on JRF.

## 4. NER Gold Candidates

**Source**: Classifier test split, resumes with ≥1,500 chars.
**Selection**: 1 per category (24 total) + 12 more spread over largest
categories, seed 42.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total NER gold IDs | 36 |
| Stored in | `data/splits/ner_gold_ids.csv` |

These IDs are reserved for hand-corrected NER annotation. They are a strict
subset of the classifier test split (verified by `verify_splits.py`).

## 5. Known Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **JD category labels are weak** | Assigned by keyword title rules, not human review. Titles matching 2+ categories are excluded, but single-match may still be wrong (e.g., "Sales Consultant" → CONSULTANT). |
| **Matcher metrics are partly circular** | SBERT trained on JDs that were also categorised by title; if the category signal drives both the JD labels and resume labels, MRR/nDCG partly reflect category overlap, not true semantic fit. |
| **Header leak residual: 19.7%** | Category words appear in resume body text, not just in the header. Masking removes the explicit title but cannot remove all category vocabulary. |
| **Synthetic NER has only 301 skills** | `ner_synthetic` templates cover a limited skill vocabulary; generalisation to real resumes needs hand-corrected gold evaluation. |
| **BPO has only 22 resumes** | Minimum test count is 3; BPO classifier performance is unreliable. |
| **9 JD categories too small for matcher** | See table above; per-category matcher metrics for these categories are meaningless. |
| **JRF has only 23 distinct JDs** | Matcher external validation via Spearman on JRF is useful as a rank-order sanity check, not as a full benchmark. |
